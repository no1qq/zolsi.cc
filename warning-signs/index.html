It follows a script. That is what makes it possible to spot.

People who target children online are not improvising. They work through a sequence that is well documented and remarkably consistent, whoever they are and whichever app they use. Once you know the sequence, it becomes visible early, while it is still easy to stop.

Read this in the right spirit

This is a pattern, not a diagnosis. Plenty of kind, ordinary people are warm and generous online and mean nothing by it. One item from this list on its own means very little. What matters is the sequence, and above all the moment secrecy enters. Knowing the pattern is meant to make you calmer and quicker, not more suspicious of everyone.

Six steps, usually in this order

  1. 01

    Choosing

    They look for someone who is easy to reach and unlikely to be closely watched. Public profiles, open comment sections, game lobbies and follower lists are where this happens. A child who posts about being lonely, bored, or in conflict with their parents is more likely to be approached.

  2. 02

    Becoming a friend

    Attention, and lots of it. They reply quickly, remember details, take an interest in the things adults usually dismiss. To the child this does not feel like danger. It feels like finally being taken seriously, which is exactly why it works.

  3. 03

    Filling a gap

    Compliments, gifts, in-game currency or items, game passes, sometimes money or phone credit. Often the gap being filled is not material at all - it is being listened to, or being told they are mature for their age.

  4. 04

    Separating

    The relationship moves somewhere more private, and a wedge goes in between the child and everyone else. Your parents would not understand. Your friends would be jealous. This is just between us. This is the step that turns attention into control, and it is the clearest signal on this page.

  5. 05

    Crossing the line

    Sexual content arrives slowly enough that no single step feels like the big one. A joke, then a question, then a photo of them, then a request. Each step is small compared to the one before, which is the whole design.

  6. 06

    Keeping control

    Once there is something to lose, the tone changes. Guilt first - you wanted this, you will get me in trouble. Then threats to send the images to family, friends or the school. Some never reach this step. Those that do rely entirely on the child staying silent.

Watch for the funnel

Step four is the one to catch, because it is visible from the outside and because everything after it depends on it. It nearly always looks the same: a conversation that starts somewhere public and open moves, in stages, somewhere private where nothing is kept.

The request to move platforms is usually framed as convenience or preference: this app is better, my other account is easier, let us talk where it is quieter. On its own it is harmless and completely normal between friends. Combined with an adult, a child, and a request for secrecy, it is the sign.

Working out whether a person is real

A profile is easy to build and almost free. These checks will not catch a determined, well prepared fake, but they catch most of them in under a minute.

  • Look at the history, not the photos. A real account has a messy past: old posts, tagged photos, comments from people who clearly know them. A recently created account with a handful of very good pictures is worth doubting.
  • Check whether anyone else treats them as real. Do their friends interact with them like friends, or is every comment generic?
  • Search their picture. Right click or long press an image and use your browser’s reverse image search. If the same face appears under other names, you have your answer.
  • Notice what they avoid. Someone who will never do a live video call, never a spontaneous photo, and always has a reason why not, is telling you something.
  • Mismatched details. The language, timing and small cultural details do not fit the person they claim to be.

Age claims mean nothing

Nothing on a profile is verified. An adult can put any age on any account, and often does. Treat the stated age of someone you have not met as an unproven claim, not a fact.

The fast version, run for money

There is a second pattern that skips almost all of the above and takes hours rather than months. A convincing profile, usually presenting as a young woman, starts a flirtatious conversation and moves quickly to exchanging images. The moment an image arrives, the tone flips to threats: pay, or this goes to your followers, your family and your school. Child protection organisations have reported sharp growth in this form, and teenage boys are targeted heavily.

It is fast, it is financial, and it is run at scale by people working through many targets at once. That is also its weakness. It depends entirely on panic and isolation, and it collapses when the target stops responding and tells someone.

If this is happening right now

  1. Stop replying. Do not negotiate, explain or apologise.
  2. Do not pay. Paying marks you as someone who pays, and the demands continue.
  3. Screenshot everything, then block and report the account.
  4. Tell someone today. A parent, another adult, or a helpline from the get help page.
  5. If the person in the image was under 18, use Take It Down. If they were 18 or over, use StopNCII.org.
